Finding 2: Envelope classification and billing basis

Contract language

Both the Original Schedule and Amendment No. 1 state:

“For generic stock, the unit rate will be billed based on usage. For Client specific stock, the unit rate will be based on receipt of such stock.”

Apex's envelopes have been classified as client-specific stock and billed on receipt (purchase volume). These are standard, unbranded envelopes that meet the contract definition of generic stock:

  • Per manufacturer die line specifications on file, 7 of 8 envelope types are standard double-window with no logos, names, or client-specific printing.
  • The envelope sizes (#10, #14, 9x12) are industry-standard formats used across the financial services industry.
  • The single-window N10 LTR variant (first purchased May 2024) represents 0.09% of #10 purchases.

Classification history

This issue was first raised by Apex in June 2023. When we questioned why envelopes were being charged at 10% instead of the contractual 5%, we were told our envelopes were “Apex specific” and not generic stock. Subsequent discussions have established that Broadridge defines “client-specific” based on operational process (how envelopes are handled on the production floor), not the actual envelope itself. The contract defines classification based on whether the stock is generic or custom — not based on operational handling.

Under generic classification, billing should be based on usage rather than receipt, applying the contractual wastage surcharge and margin:

Finding 3: CPI applied to materials

Contract language

Amendment No. 1, Section 2:

“Effective January 1, 2025, fees (other than materials) may be adjusted (up or down) by Broadridge annually, by the average percentage increase or decrease of the United States Consumer Price Index for Urban Consumers (the “CPI”)… provided, however, in no case may Broadridge increase the fees by more than 4% per annum.”

Materials (paper, envelopes) are explicitly excluded from CPI adjustments.

Starting January 2025, the paper per-page rate was increased from $0.0109 to $0.0112. This increase matches the CPI formula exactly:

PeriodRate chargedContract rateDifferenceDerivation
2024 (baseline)$0.0109$0.0109—Vendor cost × 1.10 margin
Jan–Dec 2025$0.0112$0.0109+$0.0003$0.0109 × 1.0289 (CPI) = $0.0112
Jan 2026$0.0115$0.0109+$0.0006$0.0112 × 1.0271 (CPI) = $0.0115
Feb 2026+$0.0115$0.0112+$0.0003New vendor cost, still CPI-inflated

Total overcharge across 15 months (12,524,445 pages): $4,256.

As paper is a material, CPI should not be applied regardless of effective date. The contract rate for 2025 should remain $0.0109 (same as 2024). We request the paper rate be corrected and a credit issued for the difference.

The blended production wastage rate of 9.2% is 4.6x the current contractual cap of 2%. Per Section 4, the wastage surcharge is intended to cover production losses.
