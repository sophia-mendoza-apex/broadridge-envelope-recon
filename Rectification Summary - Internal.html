Background

Broadridge has been billing our envelopes as “client-specific” stock, which means we pay based on what they purchase from the supplier rather than what they actually use to mail our jobs. We first raised this in June 2023 and were told our envelopes were “Apex specific.” They are not — they are standard, unbranded, double-window envelopes with no custom printing.

After months of back and forth, Denci acknowledged in March 2026 that “yes they are standard envelopes” but maintained the classification is based on how they handle them operationally, not what the envelope actually is. The contract makes no such distinction.

Finding 1: Wrong wastage rate in 2023

The contract has two wastage rates: 10% for paper, 5% for envelopes. Throughout 2023, Broadridge charged the 10% paper rate on envelopes. Overcharge: $20,877. This is a straightforward contract error independent of the classification issue.

Finding 2: Billing on purchases instead of usage

The contract says generic stock is billed on usage and client-specific on receipt. Because Broadridge classifies our envelopes as client-specific, we pay for every envelope they order from the supplier — including 2,673,125 envelopes purchased but never used. Broadridge's own production data shows 9.2% wastage vs a 2% contract cap, confirming they routinely over-order.
